## 2. New findings: how this audience receives a new idea

**Novelty framing is bimodal by claim size, and this audience skews toward the "How" end.** Academic framing research (concrete vs. abstract pitch framing) finds radical ideas gain acceptance when framed in concrete "How" terms, while incremental ideas do better framed abstractly as "Why." A practitioner audience that already distrusts thinkpiece abstraction (per the wedge) is exactly the audience where the concrete "how" wins — skip the "why AI changes everything" preamble and open on the mechanism. ([tandfonline.com](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/14479338.2024.2363254))

**Contestation is a feature, not a bug, when the idea threatens an existing mental model.** Strategic framing research on novel-idea evolution finds ideas that disrupt an audience's existing schema provoke contestation — and that contestation is the mechanism by which the idea gets argued about and spreads, not a sign the idea failed. Translated to this audience: a post that says "prompt engineering is a distraction from management engineering" (Cluster A's stake) is doing its job if it draws disagreement in the comments, not despite it. ([sms.onlinelibrary.wiley.com](https://sms.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/sej.70012))

**What makes a technical post spread on HN/practitioner-adjacent channels, reverse-engineered from a #1 front-page case:** a short, personal-voice title with a curiosity gap ("I've acquired a new superpower" — 5 words, implicit question); a narrative opening that drops into a concrete scene in the first sentence rather than a generic framing; and a hands-on or falsifiable component (the post embedded working puzzles) that converts passive readers into commenters, which the ranking algorithm rewards. The author's own decision rule: "if I'm the reader, what compels engagement?" — reader-centric over formula-chasing. ([danielwirtz.com](https://danielwirtz.com/blog/successful-hacker-news-article))

**Authenticity is explicitly named as the scarce resource in an AI-saturated feed.** The same case study flags that lived, personal, hard-to-replicate detail is becoming the actual differentiator as more content is visibly AI-assisted — which converges exactly with the reader panel's "AI-polished cadence kills the premise for a novelist" finding already in the Publicist research. This is now corroborated from a second, independent direction (HN craft, not reader-panel simulation).

**Sticky-idea mechanics (Heath & Heath, still the standing framework cited across marketing/virality literature): simple, unexpected, concrete, credible, emotional, story-form.** The clusters already drafted score well against this: Cluster A's "pull-cord top / dreidel scale of manager autonomy" is a concrete, slightly unexpected visual metaphor for an abstract claim — that's the sticky mechanism working, not decoration. ([Heath & Heath summary](https://18636251.s21i.faiusr.com/61/ABUIABA9GAAgzcquuQYowMibkgM.pdf))

**Naming the idea compounds distribution — but only if the name survives contact with skeptics.** swyx's "AI Engineer" (2023) and "Learn in Public" (2018) both function less as taglines than as load-bearing nouns other people now use unprompted, which is the actual signal a coined term worked (people cite it without citing the source). Cluster B's working thesis ("orchestration-as-a-service will commoditize the floor; your harness is your taste, and taste compounds") has a nameable noun candidate ("taste compounds" / "the floor") worth testing as a repeatable phrase across the series, not just this one post. ([swyx.io](https://swyx.io/))

## 3. Craft patterns, dissected

**Simon Willison — the "guide" format and radical specificity.** Willison's posts (and his newer "TIL," "notes," and "guide" content types) are organized around a strict content taxonomy, each type signaling a different commitment level to the reader (a note is low-commitment, a guide is a living reference). The craft lesson for this project: don't make every podcast cluster into the same essay shape — the series needs at least one lower-commitment, frequently-updated format (a running "signals we're tracking" note) alongside the flagship essays, so the cadence doesn't depend entirely on 2,000-word production. ([simonwillison.net](https://simonwillison.net/tags/blogging/))

**swyx — essay as community-founding document, not just explainer.** "The Rise of the AI Engineer" didn't describe a trend, it created a vocabulary other people adopted (the podcast, conference, and newsletter all grew out of defending and extending that one essay's claim). Craft lesson: the essay's "opinion stake" line already drafted for each cluster (e.g., "management theory transfers to agents almost unchanged") should be written as a claim other practitioners will want to either cite or argue against by name — not softened into a observation.

**Lenny Rachitsky — the repurposing loop, inverted for this project's favor.** Lenny's workflow starts from raw interview material → patterns/frameworks → draft → visuals, and a separate short-form loop clips 1–2 minute segments into LinkedIn posts and newsletter snippets from the same long-form source. His hook formulas skew toward loss-aversion and reader-specific stakes ("Most [role]s get this completely wrong," "we almost shut down the company because of this"). Two actionable craft rules extracted: (1) lead the post with the win/concrete finding in 1–2 sentences before any setup — don't bury the coach-loop result under throat-clearing; (2) a tactical breakdown (3–5 bullets) belongs mid-post as the skimmable spine, with the narrative wrapped around it, not instead of it. ([startupgtm.substack.com](https://startupgtm.substack.com/p/the-complete-lenny-rachitsky-playbook))

**The HN #1-post case study, as a fourth example of hook craft.** Already summarized in §2 — repeating only the structural takeaway here: title does the work of a thesis statement in five words or fewer, and the piece opens in medias res on a concrete moment ("Yesterday, I was browsing Reddit...") rather than a framing paragraph. Jeremy and Turner's clusters already have this moment available (the Inception-totem bit, the army-captain-meeting-audit) — the craft fix is making sure the post's first sentence IS that scene, not a sentence about the scene.

## 4. Size/shape prescription for a podcast-derived Substack post, this audience

- **Word count: 1,400–2,200 words**, landing inside but at the low end of the 2,000+ performance band — long enough to earn the ~2.3x reaction multiplier, short enough that a single-cluster, single-stake post doesn't pad. Don't stretch a 15-minute transcript segment past its real idea density just to hit a number; a tight 1,400 beats a padded 2,400.
- **Opening (first ~150 words): drop straight into the concrete scene from the tape — no "in the age of AI" framing, no abstract setup.** This is both the HN craft lesson and the single most-repeated reader-panel kill condition. The scene should be specific enough to be a pull-quote on its own.
- **Section rhythm, roughly five beats** (mirrors both Lenny's tactical-breakdown spine and the clusters as already drafted): (1) the concrete moment/scene, (2) the stall or problem it reveals, (3) the mechanism/fix as a skimmable 3–5-bullet breakdown, (4) one piece of outside evidence or counter-example that earns the "receipts" bar (a transcript fragment, a specific number, a named tool/API), (5) the opinion stake stated as a citable claim, not a hedge.
- **Excerpt usage: one embedded audio chapter (the pre-planned ~7–8 minute cut) placed after the concrete-scene opener, not before it** — the reader should already be hooked by prose before being offered the option to listen; the chapter timestamp is also the cross-link anchor back to the excerpt episode.
- **Never present the post as "notes from our podcast."** The transcript is raw material, fully rewritten into essay prose per the clean-up-pass discipline (preserve the logical flow and the hesitation/uncertainty that makes it feel lived-in, but restructure spoken circularity into linear argument) — this is the difference between "repurposed scraps" and an essay that happens to have a companion episode.
- **Title/hook rules:** 5–13 words, personal-voice or first-person where possible (+30% per the 94k dataset), no "X Is All You Need" or any snowclone, no question-mark ending (−6.7%), concrete noun over abstract claim. Prefer the pattern already working in the drafts — "The Overnight Shift," "Your Client Already Asked WebMD" — over the one flagged as weak ("Your AI Workforce Doesn't Need Better Prompts").
- **Cover image mandatory** (81% penalty for skipping it) — a still from the actual session, not stock art, consistent with the receipts-first positioning.
- **Pull-quotes: 2–3 standalone, extractable sentences per post**, written to survive with zero context (the opinion-stake line is usually one of them) — these are the units that travel to X/LinkedIn independent of the full post.

## 5. Post-crafting checklist (apply mechanically at the writing stage)

1. **Scene-first open.** First 150 words are the concrete moment from the tape, not a framing paragraph. Delete any sentence starting "In the age of AI..." or "As AI reshapes..."
2. **Length check: 1,400–2,200 words.** If under, is there a second concrete scene or receipt to add — not padding? If over, cut setup, not evidence.
3. **Title scrub:** 5–13 words, no snowclone format, no question mark, concrete noun, first-person where natural.
4. **Receipts present:** at least one specific number, named tool/API, or verbatim transcript fragment appears before the halfway mark.
5. **Tactical spine:** one 3–5 bullet breakdown exists and could stand alone as a skim.
6. **Opinion stake is a claim, not a hedge** — read it aloud; if it could be agreed with by everyone, rewrite it to be arguable.
7. **Zero AI-polished cadence:** scan for em-dash chains, "it's not X, it's Y" constructions, and tidy triads; if present, roughen the prose back toward how it was actually said.
8. **Cover image attached**, pulled from the actual session (not stock/generated art unrelated to the tape).
9. **2–3 pull-quotes marked** that would survive as standalone X/LinkedIn posts with zero surrounding context.
10. **Excerpt chapter embedded after the scene-opener**, with a timestamp link; full episode linked once in the footer.
11. **No "notes from our podcast" framing anywhere** — if a sentence refers to "this episode" instead of stating the finding directly, rewrite it as a finding.
12. **Cross-link check:** does this post deep-link the correct chapter of the excerpt episode, and does the excerpt episode's show notes link back to this post?
