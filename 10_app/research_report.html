1 · What this show is

Two practitioners — a corporate AI operator and an independent consultant — compare notes every two weeks on actually running AI workforces: agent fleets, factories, verification, tooling. It is a peer-discussion show, not an interview show and not a news show. That distinction drives every structural choice below.

3 · The AI podcast landscape — where the open lane is

The successful AI shows sort into four lanes:

LaneWho's winningStructure
Daily briefThe AI Daily BriefShort, single-host news digest, every day
Long-form interviewDwarkesh Podcast, Latent Space, Lex FridmanDeeply-prepped host × prominent guest, 1–3 hrs
Panel news dissectionMixture of Experts (IBM), All-InRecurring cast reacts to the week's news in timestamped topical blocks; All-In opens with banter and closes on a signature segment ("Science Corner")
Peer discussionThis Day in AI (the Sharkey brothers)Two practitioners talk for ~an hour about their own "thoroughly middle-of-the-road adventures with AI" — hands-on experience, not punditry. Chaptered after the fact, not scripted before.

The peer-discussion lane is the least crowded and it's exactly what you already do. This Day in AI proves the demand: two guys comparing real notes on real tools sustains an audience for years. What you'd add over them: you're both running actual agent workforces on real businesses — the Aug 15 call has more operational specificity than most published AI podcasts. Jeremy's own line from the call is the positioning statement: "I don't know many people who are actually having these types of conversations." The All-In structure (banter open → topical blocks → signature closer) is the best skeleton for a recurring two-cast show; This Day in AI is the tone comp.

7 · Recommendations, in order

  1. Change almost nothing about the conversation. The default discussion is the product. The structure above is landmarks around it, adding maybe 20 minutes of light intentionality to a call you're having anyway.
  2. Adopt only two segments for the next recording: the machine-titled cold open (zero prep, pure charm) and Homework (2 minutes, creates the serialized hook). Let the rest stay implicit for one episode.
  3. Prep "Since Last We Spoke" as a shared note — 3–4 bullets each, assembled in the days before the call. This is the only pre-work the format asks for, and it's exactly what the auto-generated flow (the other pane) can eventually produce for you.
  4. Name the Main Thread before recording. One sentence, agreed by text. It's the difference between "a call" and "an episode" — and it gives every episode a title, a thumbnail, and a clip strategy.
  5. Chapter in post, not in person. Like This Day in AI and Mixture of Experts: record naturally, then let the processing flow mark the segment boundaries from the transcript. Segment discipline should live in the edit, not in your heads mid-conversation.
  6. Bank the aphorisms. Every "4K→1080p" line is a clip, an episode title, or a piece of merch-grade identity. Add an extraction step to the transcript-processing flow.
  7. One open decision for you two: episode length. The call ran ~90 min; the comps run 55–75. Recommendation: aim the run of show at ~70 and let great Main Threads run long rather than padding short ones.
