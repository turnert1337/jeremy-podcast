Prepared 2026-08-29 Situation: episodes so far recorded off a literal phone call. Jeremy has a good mic and will use it. Your options: AirPods, Logitech camera, Plaud Note Pro.
The strategy in one line. Stop recording the call and start recording each side of the call locally — the "double-ender." A phone line crushes both voices into one telephone-bandwidth track; a double-ender captures two studio-quality tracks no matter how bad the connection is. Everything below serves that one move.

1 · Why the phone call recording can't be the product

A phone call compresses voice to roughly telephone bandwidth (~8 kHz), mixes both speakers onto one track, and bakes in every network hiccup. That's fine for practice tapes and transcripts — it cannot be fixed in post. Separate local tracks give you: full-bandwidth voice, independent volume/EQ per speaker, the ability to cut crosstalk, and immunity to connection quality.

This one purchase takes your side from "acceptable" to "matches Jeremy." (The $250 Shure MV7 is the same idea with polish — Pocketbook Vote it if you're feeling it.)

4 · The recording recipe for the next call

  1. Before: Riverside studio link sent. Mac on Do Not Disturb, phone silenced. Room door closed. Plaud charged.
  2. Inputs check (both of you, out loud): you — input: Logitech (or the new dynamic mic), output: AirPods. Jeremy — his mic, his headphones. Riverside shows each person's local levels; watch yours bounce as you speak.
  3. Start Plaud on the desk — it runs the whole session regardless of what the platform does.
  4. Roll: hit record in Riverside; both confirm "recording" verbally (that confirmation is on both local tracks — it's your sync proof). One sharp clap or "3-2-1" gives an alignment spike for post.
  5. Mic discipline: constant distance (a fist-and-a-half from mouth to mic), don't lean away on laughs — that's where good takes die.
  6. After: stay on the call until Riverside shows both uploads at 100% — leaving early truncates the local track. Then Plaud's transcript goes to the inbox for processing.
