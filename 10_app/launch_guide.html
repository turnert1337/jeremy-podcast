3 · Riverside — editing the episode, step by step

State check: raw tracks backed up ✓ (~/Desktop/riverside raw file backups/ — Magic Episode mix, ~106 min) · Magic Audio + normalize done ✓. What's left is the cut. The core concept: edit the transcript, not the waveform — in Riverside's editor, deleting words deletes that audio from every track, cleanly.

  1. Open the project → Editor tab. You'll see the timeline plus the transcript. Work in the transcript; use the timeline only for fine-trimming a cut point.
  2. Pass 1 — top & tail: delete everything before the conversation actually starts (setup chatter, "can you hear me") and everything after the goodbye. Biggest quality win per minute of effort.
  3. Pass 2 — the sweep: read the transcript start to finish and delete: false starts, retakes ("let me say that again"), interruptions (phones, deliveries), off-record asides. Do not chase every um — it's a conversation show; cut only what distracts. When in doubt, keep it.
  4. Pass 3 — silences: if there are long dead-air gaps, use silence trimming conservatively (threshold ~2s+). Natural pauses are pacing, not flaws.
  5. Pass 4 — verify the cuts: listen at 1.5× only around each cut point (a few seconds either side) — you're checking for clipped words and unnatural joins, not re-reviewing the episode.
  6. Export: final mix as WAV (archive) and MP3 (what you attach to the Substack post). Keep the raw backup untouched — it's your master.
