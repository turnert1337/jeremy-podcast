1 · Substack bio (your personal profile, not the publication About page)

Publicist logic: this bio shows up next to your Notes, comments, and recommendations across all of Substack. Its one job is to make a stranger who liked one comment click through to the show. Short beats clever; one identity beats three.

Version A (recommended — practitioner + show + author credit, no links competing):

Building with AI agents in practice, not in theory. Co-host of In Practice with Jeremy & Turner. Author of The Shooter Act.

Version B (show-only, tightest):

Co-host of In Practice with Jeremy & Turner: two practitioners comparing notes on running real AI workforces. Every two weeks.

"In practice, not in theory" is doing deliberate work in Version A — it's the show title turned into an identity line, and it's the phrase I'd repeat verbatim on Twitter (item 4) so the two profiles rhyme. That's how a one-person brand gets built: same sentence, everywhere, until people can finish it for you.

2 · Colors — accent yes, background no

Background: leave it default (none). Tinted backgrounds read as a newsletter trying too hard, and your cover art already carries all the warmth (its paper tone is #ECE9E2 — the site's default white lets it pop instead of matching it into mush).

Accent: the record LED. I sampled your actual cover art — the LED is #FF260D. Use it as the accent (Subscribe buttons, links). It's literally your brand mark: the tally light that's on the cover, on the pane, in the show's DNA. If it feels too hot on a big Subscribe button, one notch down is #D9220C. Same accent on both the publication (Settings → Website → theme) and your profile (Edit profile → Branding).

6 · Apple Podcasts & Spotify — how the show actually gets there

Hard prerequisite for both: one published episode. A feed with zero live episodes is rejected. So this whole item fires after Jeremy's go + your publish tap — it's the first ⚪ queue item, and both halves work fine from a phone browser if you get bored on the trip.

Spotify — use Substack's built-in sync, never manual RSS. Substack has a direct Spotify integration: Settings → your podcast (In Practice) → distribution → connect/sync to Spotify. Critical warning from Substack's own docs: once synced, do NOT also submit the RSS feed manually at Spotify for Podcasters — that creates a duplicate show you'll have to get support to kill.

Apple — manual, one form, then wait. ① Settings → your podcast → copy the RSS feed URL ② go to podcastsconnect.apple.com, sign in with your Apple ID ③ "Add a show with an RSS feed" → paste the URL → submit. Apple reviews new shows (typically a few days); you get an email when it's live, and from then on every Substack publish propagates automatically. Nothing to maintain.
