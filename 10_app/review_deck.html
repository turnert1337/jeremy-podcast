You're writing these yourself. Here's the game.

Condensed from the audience-response brief (20_research/01_audience_response_brief.md) — the checklist to run against each post as you write it.

  1. Scene-first open. First ~150 words are the concrete moment from the tape, not a framing paragraph. Delete any “In the age of AI…” sentence.
  2. 1,400–2,200 words. If a draft runs short, add a second concrete scene or receipt — not padding. If long, cut setup, not evidence.
  3. Zero AI-polished cadence. No em-dash chains, no “it's not X, it's Y” constructions, no tidy triads. Roughen the prose back toward how it was actually said.
  4. Opinion stake is a claim, not a hedge. Read it aloud — if everyone would agree with it, rewrite it to be arguable.
  5. Receipts before the halfway mark. At least one specific number, named tool/API, or verbatim transcript fragment.
  6. 2–3 pull-quotes marked that would survive as standalone X/LinkedIn posts with zero surrounding context.
  7. Never frame it as “notes from our podcast.” If a sentence refers to “this episode” instead of stating the finding directly, rewrite it as a finding.
  8. Embed the excerpt chapter after the hook, not before it — the reader should already be hooked by prose before being offered the option to listen.
  9. Title rules: 5–13 words, first-person where natural, no “X Is All You Need” snowclone, no question-mark ending.
  10. Cover image mandatory — a real still from the actual session, not stock or generated art.
