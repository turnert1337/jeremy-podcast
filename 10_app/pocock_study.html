Pocock's YouTube channel grew from 11,900 subscribers (Nov 2022) to 399,000 (Oct 2026) in four distinct phases: a 6.5x breakout year riding the Total TypeScript launch (2022–23), a long plateau typical of a mature single-topic channel (2024–early 2026, roughly +2,300 subs/month), and then a sharp re-acceleration beginning February 2026 that added more subscribers in eight months (+247,000) than the prior three-plus years combined.

The honest version of "why" is: the trigger is an open question, not a solved one. The two candidates either research pass reached for — AI Hero's record-sized cohort (enrollment opened May 17, 2026) and the skills repo going viral (first spike, April 27, 2026) — both happen after the inflection is already visible in the archival data (152K on Feb 11, 2026 → already 169K by Mar 29, before either event). A second fact-check pass caught this as a confirmed timing mismatch, not just an unproven claim: Wayback snapshots of aihero.dev from Feb 11 and Mar 1, 2026 show no cohort being promoted at all. The only event that precedes the inflection's start is the mattpocock/skills repo's creation on Feb 3, 2026 — but a repo merely existing, before any virality, is a weak mechanism for moving a subscriber count on a different platform. AI Hero itself is not new in 2026 regardless — it launched in 2025, with a 1,000-person first cohort in July 2025 that Pocock called his "most successful product launch ever" at the time; the 2026 date belongs to a later, larger, 2,500+-student cohort, not the product's origin. That correction stands, but it no longer doubles as a causal explanation for the inflection — no source found identifies what actually triggered the March 2026 acceleration.

What this means for a growth playbook: Pocock didn't get a lucky spike, he ran the exact same free-top-of-funnel-then-structured-paid-product playbook twice — Total TypeScript (2022–23, book + course) and AI Hero (2025–26, email course + cohorts + skills repo) — and the second run is now outperforming the first by a wide margin, on a longer runway of pre-seeded audience (the AI Hero newsletter was already at "3,000+ developers" in April 2025, over a year before the visible inflection). The skills repo is the most structurally interesting new piece: it's a fifth funnel leg (alongside X, YouTube, newsletter, courses) that functions as marketing, documentation, and product simultaneously, and it reaches working engineers who will never watch a YouTube tutorial.

Two things this study will not do, because the evidence doesn't support them: draw a causal arrow from any single dated event to the YouTube inflection, or chart his X/Twitter follower count. Both are explained in §2.

Why X, LinkedIn & TikTok aren't charted Turner's own mental model of Pocock is probably anchored on X — that's a reasonable instinct, Pocock's "Ultimate TypeScript Thread" and tip format did build his original reputation there. But the evidence for a chartable X trend doesn't exist: X/Twitter profile pages have been pure client-rendered single-page apps for the entire archival window (earliest capture, May 2022), so no follower count ever appears in the static HTML a Wayback snapshot can read. Live scraping is blocked (403/Cloudflare), and no third-party tracker (Social Blade, Trackalytics, Nitter mirrors) has public archived data for this handle. The only numbers available are four different search-engine cache snippets for "today" — 287,400 / 268,900 / 330,567 / 228,500 — mutually inconsistent, undated relative to each other, and impossible to reconcile into one point, let alone a series. LinkedIn (~73K) and TikTok (~13.7K) have the same problem in miniature: a single undateable current-snapshot figure each, Grade D, no independent corroboration. Charting any of these as a line would manufacture a trend that doesn't exist in the sourceable record. If X matters to the growth story — and directionally it probably does, especially pre-2023 — it has to be argued qualitatively (see §3), not plotted.

Numbered "TypeScript Tip #N" posts and ~2-minute videos, each isolating exactly one mental model. His most externally-recognized format — other sites curate it as a genre.

A full 16-chapter book, an email course, a GitHub repo — each explicitly "free forever," doing trust-building before any paywall appears.

The multi-year anti-enums campaign: one defensible-but-debatable technical stance, restated across formats and news hooks for three-plus years. Disagreement is the distribution mechanism, not a risk to it.

The skills repo is simultaneously marketing, documentation, and product — a living artifact that reaches working engineers who'd never watch a tutorial.

Pocock's own framing for the pivot, per the Pragmatic Engineer interview (paraphrased from aggregated coverage, not a verbatim quote): as AI absorbed syntax-level programming knowledge, demand for "how do I write this type" content shrank while demand for judgment-level skills — specs, process, directing agents — grew. He calls this a knowledge vs. wisdom split and repointed his entire teaching apparatus at the wisdom side. Audience reaction, by his own account and by proxy metrics (1,000-person first cohort, the self-described best launch ever, a repo that out-stars React), reads as strongly positive; no organized pushback was located in this research pass, though that's an absence-of-evidence finding, not a confirmed absence of any.

Numbered Tip #N / 2-minute videos — one isolated mental model per unit, endlessly repackable across formats.

The 2–3 standalone pull-quotes per post and the ~7–8 minute excerpt-episode chapters already specified in the audience brief and the "exhaust plume" cross-link plan are the direct equivalent. Keep treating each clip as a complete, isolated unit — not a highlight reel.

The audience brief's own Simon Willison craft lesson calls for exactly this: a lower-commitment, frequently-updated format so cadence doesn't depend entirely on 1,400–2,200-word essay production. A running X thread or short Substack Note logging raw practitioner observations between full posts.

Already underwayEffort: Low

Turner translation

The cross-link lattice is already designed (excerpt episode → each post → footer link → show notes → back to readlossy). The discipline ask is just: never let a clip or post skip the explicit "subscribe" close, the way Pocock never does.

4
Controversy as content → yes, but scoped away from Walmart
⚠ Employer-discretion flagEffort: Low
Pocock mechanic

A multi-year, repeated, defensible-but-debatable technical stance (anti-enums) that uses disagreement as the distribution mechanism, restated across formats and news hooks for years.

Turner translation

This transfers cleanly at the craft level — stakes like "prompt engineering is a distraction from management engineering" are exactly this move, and the audience-response research independently confirms contestation is a feature, not a bug, for this readership. The collision: Turner works at Walmart. The mechanic must stay scoped to agent-management/harness-building theory, never drift into anything readable as a statement about Walmart's AI strategy, vendors, or internal practice. Recommend adopting the pattern, not the specific stance, and treating "could this be read as about my employer" as a standing filter before any spiky post ships.

⚠ IP-lock flagEffort: High

Turner translation

The nearest available equivalent is something small and genuinely shareable pulled off the podcast's build-your-own-harness thread — a prompt pack, a call-sheet template, a standalone "coach loop" skill file. Explicitly NOT the Dev Command Center frame itself: the DCC IP posture is locked to outbound-donations-only per standing operator decision, and this recommendation does not reopen that question.
