The Podcast: Format Research & Content Plan

What the shows we like actually do structurally, where the open lane is in the AI podcast landscape, and a proposed run of show — built from what our Aug 15 call already does naturally.

Prepared 2026-08-29 Inputs: reference-show brief · full Aug 15 call transcript · web research (cited at bottom)
The one-paragraph read. We already have a show — the Aug 15 call breaks cleanly into six recognizable segments without either of us trying. The recommendation is not to script anything: it's to name the segments that already happen, add one 8-minute news block that the 2-week cadence makes natural (the Morning Brew move), and adopt one signature cold-open bit no other podcast can do (the machine titles our episode; we read it cold). Everything else stays a normal conversation.

1 · What this show is

Two practitioners — a corporate AI operator and an independent consultant — compare notes every two weeks on actually running AI workforces: agent fleets, factories, verification, tooling. It is a peer-discussion show, not an interview show and not a news show. That distinction drives every structural choice below.

3 · The AI podcast landscape — where the open lane is

The successful AI shows sort into four lanes:

LaneWho's winningStructure
Daily briefThe AI Daily BriefShort, single-host news digest, every day
Long-form interviewDwarkesh Podcast, Latent Space, Lex FridmanDeeply-prepped host × prominent guest, 1–3 hrs
Panel news dissectionMixture of Experts (IBM), All-InRecurring cast reacts to the week's news in timestamped topical blocks; All-In opens with banter and closes on a signature segment ("Science Corner")
Peer discussionThis Day in AI (the Sharkey brothers)Two practitioners talk for ~an hour about their own "thoroughly middle-of-the-road adventures with AI" — hands-on experience, not punditry. Chaptered after the fact, not scripted before.

The peer-discussion lane is the least crowded and it's exactly what we already do. This Day in AI proves the demand: two guys comparing real notes on real tools sustains an audience for years. What we'd add over them: we're both running actual agent workforces on real businesses — the Aug 15 call has more operational specificity than most published AI podcasts. Jeremy's own line from the call is the positioning statement: "I don't know many people who are actually having these types of conversations." The All-In structure (banter open → topical blocks → signature closer) is the best skeleton for a recurring two-cast show; This Day in AI is the tone comp.

5 · Proposed run of show (~70 min, biweekly)

SegmentTimeWhat it is
1The Machine Titles the Show2–3 minThe signature cold open, and it's ours alone: the transcription AI auto-titles the recording, and we read its title cold, then react. Born on-air Aug 15 ("Two Geniuses Discuss Genius Things"). No other show can do this bit honestly — it is the show's thesis (AI in the loop) as comedy.
2Since Last We Spoke8–10 minThe Morning Brew move fitted to our cadence: 3–4 quick news bites from the last two weeks, filtered hard — only what touches our active threads (agents, tooling, observability). Ends with a paired mini-bit, e.g. Pocketbook Vote / Refund of the Week: one tool one of us paid for, one that disappointed. (Direct descendant of Stock/Dog of the Week.)
3The Main Thread30–35 minThe default discussion, untouched. One big question, worked end-to-end. Only structural addition: name the question out loud in the first minute ("today's thread: when do you hire a human?") so the episode is titleable and clippable.
4I'm Stealing That8–10 minFormalizes what already happens: each of us names the one idea from the other we're taking home and how we'll use it. Great recurring payoff — next episode opens the loop: did the steal work?
5Canary Corner~5 minOne wild/alarming/strange-but-true AI story (the GPT-escape slot). Named for Jeremy's dead-man-switch project — the segment where we check if the canary is still singing. Our "Science Corner" analog.
6Homework~5 minCloser: each host commits to one thing to test before next call; unresolved threads logged out loud. Plus the Whiteboard micro-bit: the one aphorism of the episode, restated ("4K → 1080p"). Builds episode-to-episode continuity — the serialized hook most discussion shows lack.

6 · Who our listener is (topic adjacency)

Someone who'd enjoy the Aug 15 conversation is an operator/builder using AI on a real business, not a researcher. Their categories, in order of adjacency — this doubles as the Main Thread topic bank:

  • Agentic ops — orchestration, verification, alarm panels, agent hierarchies (our home turf)
  • AI tooling hands-on — what to buy, what it costs, what broke
  • Solo/small-business AI leverage — the "hire a human or build an agent" math, SOP compression, the smart-intern doctrine
  • Business of AI consulting — client trust, verification logs as a client-facing asset, observability as "the next HubSpot"
  • Build-in-public / indie hacker culture — factories, stack managers, custom "motorcycle software"

Directory placement: Technology primary, Business secondary. Position against This Day in AI (tone) and All-In (structure), never against Dwarkesh/Latent Space (different lane — don't compete on interviews or research depth).

7 · Recommendations, in order

  1. Change almost nothing about the conversation. The default discussion is the product. The structure above is landmarks around it, adding maybe 20 minutes of light intentionality to a call we're having anyway.
  2. Adopt only two segments for the next recording: the machine-titled cold open (zero prep, pure charm) and Homework (2 minutes, creates the serialized hook). Let the rest stay implicit for one episode.
  3. Prep "Since Last We Spoke" as a shared note — 3–4 bullets each, assembled in the days before the call. The only pre-work the format asks for.
  4. Name the Main Thread before recording. One sentence, agreed by text. It's the difference between "a call" and "an episode" — and it gives every episode a title, a thumbnail, and a clip strategy.
  5. Chapter in post, not in person. Like This Day in AI and Mixture of Experts: record naturally, then mark segment boundaries from the transcript. Segment discipline should live in the edit, not in our heads mid-conversation.
  6. Bank the aphorisms. Every "4K→1080p" line is a clip, an episode title, or a piece of merch-grade identity.
  7. One open decision: episode length. The call ran ~90 min; the comps run 55–75. Recommendation: aim the run of show at ~70 and let great Main Threads run long rather than padding short ones.
